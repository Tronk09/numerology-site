      Article title — make it a real question someone would search

      Category8 Aug 2026· 6 min read

      Open the post with a sentence that names the reader's real problem. What are they about to learn, and why should they care? The intro sets the promise the rest of the post keeps.

      Section one — teach one clear idea

      One section = one idea. Short paragraphs. Plain language. You are writing to a friend who is curious about numbers, not to an academic. If a sentence can be shorter, make it shorter.

      “Pull quotes that reinforce the central idea work well — they break up the page and give scanners something to land on.”

      Use the helper styles

      These helpers are already styled — just use them:

      • Bullet lists for enumerations and quick takeaways.
      • Numbered lists for step-by-step instructions you want the reader to follow.
      • Tables for quick-reference data, like the nine numbers at a glance.
      • Callouts for “here's the key point” boxes.
      The key point This box is styled for a central takeaway — the thing that gets shared or remembered.

      A sub-heading for a narrower point

      Use h3 sparingly, under an h2. Too many levels and the article starts to feel like a wiki crawl.
